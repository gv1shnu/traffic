Which vehicle caused the jam?

A fixed CCTV camera watches a street. Traffic stops. This project detects and tracks every road user, decides whether the congestion is sustained, and ranks which subject most plausibly started it, or says unknown when the evidence does not support blaming anyone.

The footage below is rendered 3D simulation. The boxes, track IDs, congestion timing and suspected cause are produced by the real detector (YOLO11n + ByteTrack) and the unmodified investigation pipeline. Ground truth from the simulation is used only to score the result.

Across all scenarios

Detection is scored at IoU ≥ 0.5 against boxes projected from the 3D scene, for subjects at least half visible (ray-cast) and at least 20 px tall, on every frame the pipeline sampled (5 FPS). The full report is docs/sim3d-evaluation.json.

How it works

  1. Simulate and render

    A car-following traffic model with Indian mixed traffic drives a Blender scene of a two-lane street. A scripted event (a breakdown, a pedestrian in the lane, a red signal) is the known ground truth.

  2. Detect and track

    The rendered MP4 is uploaded through the app's API. YOLO11n detects cars, buses, trucks, motorcycles and people; ByteTrack links them into tracks.

  3. Measure congestion

    Relative movement, occupancy, slow-vehicle fraction and queue growth in the configured lane decide whether congestion is sustained and when it began.

  4. Attribute the cause

    Candidates are scored on precedence, position, blockage, stationary time, follower response, upstream propagation and counter-evidence such as adjacent lanes stopping at the same time.

What this does and does not show

  • It shows the full pipeline working end to end on video with exact, independently known ground truth.
  • It does not measure real-world accuracy. Rendered scenes are cleaner than real cameras: no rain, glare, night, compression artefacts or camera shake.
  • The generic COCO detector has no autorickshaw class, so autorickshaws are often missed or labelled as cars; the autorickshaw scenario shows this limitation.
  • Scores are heuristic evidence strength, not calibrated probabilities. The output is decision support for a human reviewer, never a basis for enforcement.
